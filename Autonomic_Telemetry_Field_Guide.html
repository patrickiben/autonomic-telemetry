A descriptive workbench for comparing a recording with its own reference. Every number needs its source and context.

An experimental display, with no clinical validation.

The custom scores do not measure stress, sympathetic activity, illness, or recovery. They describe selected changes in supplied summaries. They carry no clinical thresholds or confidence intervals.

01

Start with the demo

Use a known story to understand what each display can show.

  1. Open the console and keep the synthetic label in view. The scenarios are generated examples, not a person's recording.
  2. Use the Reference, Change, Easing, and Motion presets to jump to key moments, then explore with play/pause, playback speed, restart, and the timeline scrubber.
  3. Compare the index's contribution bars with the actual HR, RMSSD, and respiratory-rate traces. Notice how excluded data creates a gap instead of a confident score.

The demo supplies a deterministic eight-minute, 1 Hz series and a preloaded two-minute reference. Faster playback changes how quickly you watch it; it does not change the recorded time scale. Restart returns to the beginning of the same data.

Reference shift

How far the current summaries have moved in selected directions from a fixed comparison segment.

Novelty

How unusual the latest step is. A sustained shift can remain high on the index after novelty falls.

02

Your wearable context

Hume Band 2.0

Choose Add Hume reading to copy the date, resting heart rate, HRV, and respiratory rate available in your Hume app. For HRV, choose Unspecified / not confirmed, RMSSD, or SDNN. Keep it unspecified if the source does not identify the method. The dated reading stays in memory and travels with your export. A new reading or Health XML replaces the current context snapshot.

Hume's FAQ describes partial Apple Health/Google Fit sharing and lists native export as “Coming Soon” (checked 22 October 2026). This console assumes no direct Hume API, CSV export, or Bluetooth feed.

If your Hume metrics appear in Apple Health, load its unzipped export.xml and select the Hume source name. Check which metrics actually arrived. Google Fit import is not supported here.

Apple Watch

Load Apple Health XML and choose the Watch source. HealthKit HRV is SDNN; it is kept separate from RMSSD. The latest record is selected by measurement timestamp for each supported metric within the selected source. Each value shows its date; older than 30 days is labeled stale.

Daily context stays context. Neither manual notes nor sparse Health records enter the 1 Hz session score or seed its reference. No sleep-stage or recovery estimate is derived. Shared physiology views are device neutral.

Earlier Muse Web Bluetooth and OSC sketches have been retired. A supported live feed would require verified acquisition, timing, artifact handling, and signal-processing validation.

03

Quality before interpretation

Only samples labeled quality: "good" enter the model. poor and unknown are excluded. When motion is supplied, it must be 0.25 g or less. Motion means magnitude after gravity removal, not the raw accelerometer's roughly 1 g at rest.

A missing motion value cannot establish stillness, and “good” quality is the file provider's assertion. The console does not independently validate sensors or reconstruct quality from a raw recording.

A fixed comparison segment

A reference needs at least 60 eligible samples spanning 119 seconds, with at least two of HR, RMSSD, and respiratory rate present. A candidate restarts after an excluded sample, insufficient channels, a gap longer than 10 seconds, or a change in those available channels. Once formed, the reference stays fixed for the session; scoring starts on the next eligible sample.

Same channels, same comparison. A scored row must have the reference's channel set. A missing channel withholds the index rather than silently increasing the weights of the remaining channels.

The reference is not a health norm or proof of rest. Recording conditions, timing, and preprocessing affect the comparison. There is no saved baseline; a new recording or page reload starts fresh.

04

Read the pattern-shift index

A transparent directional composite on a 0–100 display scale.

Input Direction scored Weight SD floor
Heart rate Increase 40% 2 bpm
RMSSD Decrease 40% 5 ms
Respiratory rate Increase 20% 1 breath/min

For each reference channel, the console divides the directional difference from its reference mean by its reference SD, using the floor above when needed. Positive standardized shifts are clipped to 0–4, divided by four, then combined with the weights. Weights are normalized once for the reference's available channels.

For example, if only HR and RMSSD form the reference, each has 50% weight. Later rows keep that exact pair. EEG and novelty contribute no weight.

Score Display language
Below 25 Low directional shift
25 to below 46 Small shift
46 to below 68 Moderate shift
68–100 Large shift
Only HR increases, RMSSD decreases, and respiratory-rate increases contribute. A low score means little shift in those directions; large opposite-direction changes can also score zero. Low does not mean close to the reference, calm, or healthy. The weights, floors, clipping, and tiers are design choices, and a large shift does not identify a cause.
05

Novelty and coincident change

Novelty is the root mean square of standardized feature first differences per second, centered and scaled against the reference's rates of change. Actual timestamp intervals are used. It is dimensionless. It is not a likelihood, information in nats, a forecast, or a direct physiological measurement.

An event requires eligible data, novelty of at least 3, and at least two directional steps of one reference SD or more relative to the preceding five-second mean: HR up, RMSSD down, or respiration up. There must be at least three prior clean samples in that interval. A 20-second refractory interval separates logged events.

What it describes

A coincident, unusually large change in the supplied channels under a specific heuristic rule.

What remains unknown

Cause, physiological meaning, sensor validity, and whether a condition is present. An event is not labeled a sympathetic surge or stress episode.

Use markers to note your own context. A marker records what you typed; it does not establish causation or change the calculations. The detector's threshold has no validated false-positive rate.

06

The other lenses

Display How to read it
Trend association Pearson correlation of aligned, clean, contiguous HR and respiratory-rate summaries. It does not establish RSA, causal direction, or a physiological coupling strength.
Temporal texture · HR persistence Lag-one association of consecutive clean HR values. This is a sample lag; sampling and smoothing matter. It is not a critical-transition warning.
Temporal texture · RMSSD dispersion Sample standard deviation of the supplied RMSSD summaries. It is not beat-to-beat SDNN or autonomic complexity.
State portrait Standardized HR against standardized RMSSD relative to the fixed reference. These are the actual two axes, not PCA or a learned phenotype. Points are clipped at the frame.
Supplied EEG beta/alpha Optional value in Temporal texture. It carries no index weight or physiological interpretation.

Association and texture use the latest contiguous clean window of up to 60 seconds and need at least 20 relevant samples.

The breathing pacer

The pacer is an independent visual timing guide. It does not measure breathing, adherence, RSA, or a treatment response. Changing the pacer's speed does not modify the recording or its scores.

This release does not calculate LF/HF, spectral power, RSA amplitude, entropy, a causal network, or a critical-transition predictor.

07

Bring data. Keep its provenance.

Import already-computed summaries as normalized JSON or CSV. Start with the repository's CSV template or synthetic JSON example. The console does not derive RMSSD from raw ECG, PPG, or RR intervals. 

timestamp,heart_rate_bpm,rmssd_ms,resp_rate_brpm,eeg_beta_alpha,motion_g,quality
2026-10-22T10:00:00-04:00,62,48,12,,0.03,good

The example row demonstrates the columns; it is not enough to form a reference. Use ISO timestamps with seconds and an explicit timezone, numeric Unix epoch milliseconds in JSON, or 13-digit epoch milliseconds in CSV. Rows must be at least one second apart; the limit is 20,000 samples and 20 MB. Blank numeric cells remain missing. The JSON format is versioned with schema_version: 2; see Methods for the full contract.

CSV is labeled user-supplied; JSON must explicitly declare provenance. Neither becomes a “verified measurement.” Exports preserve provenance, missing values, timestamps, quality, markers, and context notes for the complete loaded recording. Synthetic JSON sessions remain labeled synthetic. The repository's example notes identify its six-row CSV template as synthetic format data with intentionally unknown quality.

Local processing, explicit files. The console makes no network requests and writes no browser storage. Imports stay in the current page's memory; a reload clears them. Exported JSON is a file under your control and may contain sensitive health information or marker text.

Before posting a repository, inspect its files and Git staging area. Keep personal recordings and Health exports outside it. A filename exclusion list cannot recognize every sensitive file.

08

Evidence and limits

The references below inform measurement and interpretation cautions. They do not validate this console's composite, tiers, novelty statistic, or event detector. A software test can verify arithmetic or file behavior; it cannot establish clinical accuracy.

  • Billman · 2013

    Why LF/HF should not be read as cardiac sympathovagal balance. This release omits that ratio.

  • Laborde, Mosley & Thayer · 2017

    Practical guidance on HRV study design, analysis, and reporting. HRV needs careful measurement and context.

  • Apple · HealthKit HRV SDNN

    Apple's definition of its SDNN quantity. SDNN and RMSSD remain different measures here.

Use the console for exploration and reproducible examples. Its outputs should not guide diagnosis, treatment, or decisions about a health condition.
